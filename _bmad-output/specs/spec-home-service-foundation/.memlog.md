- (decision) Adopt the existing v1 contract, schemas, and Home architecture notes as companions and traceability sources.
- (capability) CAP-1: authenticated clients read the active configuration and committed revision.
- (capability) CAP-2: administrators atomically replace the complete configuration with expected-revision conflict protection.
- (capability) CAP-3: authenticated Devices receive deterministic bounded wake-claim arbitration outcomes.
- (capability) CAP-4: malformed, late, revoked, unavailable, and unmapped claims fail closed without a Hermes turn.
- (capability) CAP-5: the service recovers the last committed configuration after restart.
- (constraint) Domain behavior must run under fake ports without a live Hermes endpoint, HTTP framework, or physical hardware.
- (direction) Implement SQLite durability, pure validation and arbitration, then a thin HTTP adapter as the next vertical slice.
- (event) Foundation spec seed drafted from the existing Home boundary and v1 contract.
- (capability by coach) CAP-6: A trusted household administrator can enroll a new endpoint from a short-lived, single-use code and explicit approval; scanning alone never grants access.
- (capability by coach) CAP-7: Home can issue, validate, expire, rotate, revoke, and re-enroll opaque per-device credentials distinct from Home admin and Hermes bearer credentials.
- (capability by coach) CAP-8: Revoking an endpoint hard-stops new capture, follow-up, Watch, and notification access and makes late device actions harmless; reachable active work is interrupted and reconnect requires fresh authorization.
- (constraint by coach) Enrollment is a two-step trust boundary: the QR or enrollment payload carries only a single-use code, and only an already trusted phone or TUI can approve the pending endpoint.
- (constraint by coach) Device credentials are externally opaque, scoped to one endpoint, and never appear in configuration snapshots, URLs, ordinary logs, diagnostics, prompts, transcripts, Watch content, or audio.
- (constraint by coach) The existing v1 Device authorization remains the session/claim authentication shape; credential lifecycle is added around it without handing a personal Hermes token to an endpoint or inventing a new Hermes channel.
- (direction by coach) Implement pairing and credential lifecycle as a Home-owned foundation slice before Profile conversation claims or the transparent Hermes bridge; keep the external behavior testable without a chosen HTTP framework.
- (assumption by coach) The pilot has one logical Household Server and at least one already trusted control-plane device capable of approving enrollment.
- (question by coach) What exact code TTL, credential lifetime, rotation overlap, revocation storage, and credential-at-rest protection should the Home implementation use?
- (question by coach) Which trusted control-plane surface approves an enrollment in the first pilot, and what endpoint identity details must the human confirm before approval?
- (question by coach) What transport and same-household identity proof must pair with roaming routes? This belongs to the later Home bridge/route slice, not the credential object itself.
- (event by coach) Pairing and per-device credentials became the first Home-owned implementation specification; the existing foundation's credential-lifecycle non-goal is now promoted into CAP-6 through CAP-8 with unresolved mechanism details kept open.
- (event by coach) Spec self-validation pass 1 (coherence): PASS. CAP-1 through CAP-8 remain stable and unique; every capability has intent and success; constraints bend implementation; non-goals are explicit; the success signal is testable; unresolved mechanism choices are open questions rather than invented defaults.
- (event by coach) Spec self-validation pass 2 (preservation): PASS. The adopted Home architecture, v1 contract, next-wave PRD, feature slate, and cross-repository architecture boundary are represented in the kernel or credential-lifecycle companion; wrapper-only narrative was omitted.
- (event by coach) Spec update finalized for the pairing and per-device credential slice; no stories.yaml was created because story breakdown is a separate interactive step and no story checkpoints were agreed.
- (decision by user) Pairing enrollment codes are valid for five minutes and can be consumed once; explicit approval by a trusted control-plane device remains required. Credential lifetime, rotation overlap, storage, and revocation persistence remain open.
- (event by coach) Spec self-validation after the user decision (coherence): PASS. The five-minute single-use enrollment-code rule is reflected consistently in CAP-6, constraints, lifecycle states, Home architecture AD-7, and open questions now limited to credential lifetime, rotation, storage, and approval UI.
- (event by coach) Spec self-validation after the user decision (preservation): PASS. The user's five-minute choice is recorded in the Home spec memlog, canonical product memlog, Home architecture memlog, SPEC.md, lifecycle companion, and feature slate; no source claim was dropped.
- (decision by user) Adopt the pilot credential policy: each endpoint credential expires 90 days after issue; Home offers automatic renewal when 14 days remain; the replaced credential is accepted for at most 10 minutes to retry a lost rotation response, and explicit revocation invalidates it immediately. A device that misses expiry must re-enroll.
- (event by coach) Spec self-validation after the user accepted credential policy (coherence): PASS. CAP-7, constraints, lifecycle states, Home AD-7, and feature-slate language agree on 90-day expiry, renewal in the final 14 days, at most 10 minutes of old-credential overlap, immediate revocation, and re-enrollment after expiry.
- (event by coach) Spec self-validation after the user accepted credential policy (preservation): PASS. The adopted lifetime and rotation policy is recorded in the Home spec, credential companion, Home architecture, canonical product memlog, and feature slate; only idempotency, storage, and approval UI remain open.
- (decision by user) Adopt renewal idempotency: the endpoint creates a rotation request ID bound to its current credential generation; retrying that same request during the 10-minute overlap returns the same replacement result and never mints another credential. A different request cannot create a second replacement, and explicit revocation invalidates the retry path.
- (event by coach) Spec self-validation after adopting renewal idempotency (coherence): PASS. CAP-7, constraints, lifecycle companion, Home AD-7, and feature-slate Slice A agree that a client-generated request ID is bound to the current credential generation, same-request retries return the same replacement, competing requests do not mint another, and revocation invalidates retries.
- (event by coach) Spec self-validation after adopting renewal idempotency (preservation): PASS. The request-ID behavior is recorded in the Home spec, lifecycle companion, Home architecture, canonical product memlog, and feature slate; only revocation persistence, credential-at-rest protection, and approval UI remain open.
- (decision by user) Adopt the secure-storage boundary: Home stores only a keyed digest of each credential, keeps the root secret outside SQLite, and retains any retryable replacement only as an encrypted short-lived record. Endpoints store the credential in platform secure storage or fail closed; plaintext config, logs, snapshots, URLs, and ordinary history are forbidden.
- (event by coach) Spec self-validation after adopting secure storage (coherence): PASS. The kernel and lifecycle companion distinguish Home keyed digests, an external root secret, short-lived encrypted rotation-retry material, and endpoint secure storage; plaintext persistence is forbidden and unresolved primitive/adapter choices remain open.
- (event by coach) Spec self-validation after adopting secure storage (preservation): PASS. The storage boundary is recorded in the Home spec, lifecycle companion, Home architecture, canonical product memlog, and feature slate; no credential or rotation requirement was dropped.
- (capability by user) CAP-9: A trusted control-plane surface can review a pending endpoint request and approve a bounded scope or reject it before any credential is issued; the first pilot surface is the TUI and phones later consume the same contract.
- (constraint by user) Enrollment approval is deny-by-default and scope-bound: only a trusted Home admin surface may approve; the endpoint cannot self-approve or grant itself capabilities, Rooms, or Profile mappings.
- (decision by user) Adopt the first approval surface as the existing TUI: it reviews endpoint identity, a short confirmation code, expiry, requested Room, capabilities, and Profile mappings; the approver selects bounded scope or rejects. Approval and credential issuance are atomic; phones later adopt the same Home contract, and a display cannot self-approve.
- (event by user) Spec self-validation after adopting the TUI approval surface (coherence): PASS. CAP-1 through CAP-9 are stable and unique; CAP-9 covers explicit bounded review, approve, and reject; constraints, lifecycle companion, Home AD-7, and feature-slate language agree that approval is deny-by-default and atomic with issuance.
- (event by user) Spec self-validation after adopting the TUI approval surface (preservation): PASS. Endpoint identity, confirmation code, deadline, Room/capability/Profile scope, approval atomicity, phone-later contract reuse, and display no-self-approval are recorded in the Home spec, lifecycle companion, Home architecture, canonical product memlog, and feature slate.
