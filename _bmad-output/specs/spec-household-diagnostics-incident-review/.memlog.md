goal: Record safe lifecycle telemetry across endpoint, Home, and Hermes and support explicit previewed incident capture without blocking or replaying live turns

- (direction) Headless express mode: distill PRD FR-51 through FR-53, the canonical feature slate, Home AD-12, TUI AD-23, and the existing Prometheus/Grafana boundary into a separate safe-telemetry and incident-review contract.
- (decision) Slice D owns the automatic diagnostics boundary and explicit incident capture; Slice C owns live bridge transport, while endpoint, Home, and ops adapters emit or review the shared contract.
- (capability) CAP-1: Every successful, failed, interrupted, and unavailable turn can produce a correlation-linked safe lifecycle record across endpoint, Home, and Hermes bridge.
- (capability) CAP-2: The household can review low-cardinality metrics and structured event timelines for a turn without putting content-bearing data into automatic metrics.
- (capability) CAP-3: An endpoint can report safe telemetry state, last successful upload, queued-event count, collector reachability, and retention deadline.
- (capability) CAP-4: The system can hold a bounded local pre-failure ring buffer for one endpoint and current task or session without uploading it automatically.
- (capability) CAP-5: An authorized person can preview and explicitly approve a bounded incident capture for one endpoint and current task or session before encrypted upload.
- (capability) CAP-6: The diagnostics path can retain, preserve, delete, and audit diagnostic records without blocking, mutating, or replaying a live Hermes turn.
- (decision) Automatic safe telemetry is emitted for healthy as well as failed work. The common envelope links endpoint, Home, and Hermes with one opaque correlation ID and records phase transitions, durations, route, versions, byte counts, health results, and typed outcomes or failures.
- (decision) Automatic diagnostics exclude prompts, transcripts, audio, credentials, keys, Sensitive Entry values, and private notification content; identifiers are opaque or non-reversible. The schema rejects forbidden fields rather than relying on operator intent.
- (decision) Metrics and detailed structured timelines remain separate stores and review shapes. Metrics are low-cardinality and use the existing ops/Grafana boundary; content-bearing data does not enter metrics.
- (decision) Explicit incident capture is scoped to one endpoint and its current task or session. The local 60-second pre-failure ring buffer remains ephemeral until preview and approval, and the saved bundle is encrypted, separately transported, and visibly retained for seven days unless preserved.
- (decision) Safe metrics retain for 30 days and detailed structured events retain for 14 days. Preservation and deletion are explicit operations and apply to the review path, not merely to dashboard visibility.
- (decision) Telemetry is best-effort. Local queues are bounded, loss is visible, collector or storage outage does not block, change, retry, or replay a live Hermes turn, and incident upload failure does not change the live Session.
- (constraint) The Home service owns correlation, authorization, redaction policy, retention state, and diagnostics status; clients and ops adapters do not create competing diagnostic authorities.
- (constraint) Automatic event and metric schemas must reject prompt text, transcript text, audio bytes, credentials, keys, Sensitive Entry values, private notification content, and reversible household identifiers.
- (constraint) Every automatic record identifies whether it is a metric, structured event, queue/loss signal, health result, or incident state; automatic telemetry never smuggles detailed content through a supposedly safe field.
- (constraint) The current task or session and one endpoint are the maximum scope of an explicit incident capture. A capture cannot silently expand to another Room, Profile, endpoint, or historical transcript.
- (constraint) Incident capture uses a separate encrypted diagnostics path and never modifies the Standard Hermes Channel or live turn state.
- (constraint) Retention targets are fixed at 30 days for safe metrics, 14 days for detailed event logs, and 7 days for incident bundles unless an authorized person preserves a bundle.
- (constraint) Remote transport and stored diagnostic data require protection and auditable access; deletion must remove records from the review path rather than merely hiding them from a dashboard.
- (constraint) Diagnostics status is visible to the endpoint and operators, including enabled state, last successful upload, queued count, collector reachability, and retention deadline.
- (constraint) A bounded local queue or ring buffer may lose data under outage or capacity pressure, but the loss must be observable and must never become a reason to delay or replay a Hermes turn.
- (non-goal) Automatically exporting prompts, transcripts, audio, credentials, keys, Sensitive Entry values, or private notifications to remote analytics.
- (non-goal) Keeping detailed conversation evidence indefinitely or turning the household service into a public multi-household observability platform.
- (non-goal) Choosing the final structured-log backend, encrypted bundle store, cryptographic primitives, trusted-surface role model, or audio buffer encoding in this kernel.
- (non-goal) Replacing the ordinary Hermes channel, adding remote desktop behavior, or making diagnostics a second session/control channel.
- (non-goal) Building a fleet dashboard before the single-device status and current-task incident path works.
- (assumption) The existing Home Prometheus/Grafana path remains the first review boundary, while the final log and encrypted-bundle stores are selected in the owning implementation slice.
- (assumption) Slice A supplies per-device authorization and Slice C supplies the live session/route identity needed to create a correlation-linked diagnostic timeline.
- (assumption) An endpoint can stage a bounded local ring buffer and can show a preview before any selected detailed content leaves the household.
- (assumption) A trusted control-plane surface exists to authorize capture and later preserve or delete a bundle; the exact surfaces and roles remain open.
- (question) Which structured log backend and encrypted incident-bundle store extend the existing ops/Grafana path, and what transport protection do they require?
- (question) Which trusted surfaces and household roles may start, preview, preserve, and delete incident bundles?
- (question) Which evidence categories are selected by default for a bundle, and how are detailed prompts, transcripts, and audio previewed without leaking them before approval?
- (question) What exact bounded ring-buffer contents, audio encoding, local quota, and overwrite behavior are safe for each endpoint class?
- (question) Which component enforces the automatic safe-event schema at each boundary, and how are redaction failures surfaced without blocking the live turn?
- (question) What upload retry, backpressure, and queue-loss policy makes the last successful upload and dropped-event count trustworthy during a prolonged outage?
- (question) What audit record and remote-erasure evidence prove that an explicit deletion removed data from the review path?
- (event) Slice D contract seed recorded. Open questions are limited to backend and transport choices, trusted capture roles, evidence selection and preview, ring-buffer format and quota, redaction enforcement, outage queue policy, and deletion evidence.
- (event) Spec self-validation pass 1 (coherence): PASS. CAP-1 through CAP-6 each have intent and testable success; automatic versus explicit capture is an enforceable design boundary; non-goals are explicit; retention and outage rules are concrete; unresolved backend, role, preview, buffer, redaction, queue, and deletion choices remain open rather than invented.
- (event) Spec self-validation pass 2 (preservation): PASS. PRD FR-51 through FR-53, the accepted remote-debugging direction, Home AD-12, TUI AD-23, the three-layer metrics/timeline/bundle model, 60-second buffer, preview and encryption boundary, 30/14/7-day retention, visible endpoint status, schema rejection, audit, deletion, and no-blocking behavior are represented in the kernel or diagnostics-contract companion.
